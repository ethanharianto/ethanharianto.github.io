I spent a quarter studying RL-based prompt compression and came out of it
believing something I didn't before: in applied RL on LLMs, the reward
function is the thing. Everything else is plumbing.

The setup was simple enough. A frozen DistilRoBERTa encoder, a small trainable
head, and a policy that emits per-token keep/drop decisions. Reward was
derived from a downstream LLM's answer on the compressed prompt. Two
algorithms, two reward schemes, three learning rates — a 2×2×2 factorial,
because it was small and cheap.

## The part I was wrong about

Going in, I assumed the interesting axis was algorithm. REINFORCE vs. MCTS
felt like where the engineering would sit.

It wasn't. Two reward pathologies dominated the result:

1. **Per-token KL rewards punish dropping tokens harder than keeping them.**
   You can prove this on paper with a few lines of algebra. In practice, it
   collapses the policy to "keep everything" in the first 200 episodes and
   never recovers.
2. **Comparing the compressed-prompt answer to ground-truth hides faithfulness
   drift.** If the uncompressed model was wrong and the compressed model is
   also wrong, you reward faithfulness. What you actually want is: "did the
   behavior change under compression?" — which is a comparison against the
   uncompressed model, not against truth.

Both are obvious in retrospect. Neither was obvious at the start.

## Why this generalizes

Production LLM pipelines are, increasingly, RL problems in disguise. Anywhere
there's a router, a fallback, a compressor, a retriever, or a tool picker,
there's an implicit reward. Often it's "did the user accept the output?" —
which is exactly the ground-truth-collapse case above, except now the
ground-truth is a human who doesn't know what they don't know.

If I only took one thing away: before you pick an algorithm, write down the
reward and stare at it. Ask:

- What fixed point does this reward incentivize?
- What happens if the policy is lazy? Adversarial?
- Does the reward change when I change *nothing* about the underlying answer
  quality?

In my case, answering the third question honestly would have saved weeks.
