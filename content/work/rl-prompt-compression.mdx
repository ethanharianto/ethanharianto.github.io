<Kicker>Motivation</Kicker>

## Dropping tokens is cheap. Dropping the *right* tokens is not.

Prompt compression — dropping tokens from a long prompt while preserving
downstream task performance — is a direct lever on LLM cost and latency. The
supervised state-of-the-art (LLMLingua-2) trains a classifier on token-level
labels. We studied a different question: can reinforcement learning *discover*
which tokens to drop, purely from task reward, without per-token supervision?

## Setup

A frozen DistilRoBERTa encoder produces token embeddings; a small trainable
head emits binary keep/drop decisions. The compressed prompt is fed to a
frozen LLM to produce an answer. Reward is computed from the LLM's
downstream answer, not per-token labels.

We studied two algorithm families and two reward schemes in a 2×2×2
factorial (learning rate as the third axis):

- **Algorithms**: REINFORCE and MCTS.
- **Rewards**: harmonic (balancing compression and faithfulness) and a
  teacher-forced cross-entropy signal used as a dense surrogate.

## Two reward pathologies we hit — and fixed

We found two concrete reward-design bugs that dominated the result.

### 1. Keep/drop asymmetry in per-token KL

A naive per-token KL reward punishes *dropping* tokens asymmetrically vs.
*keeping* them. Early in training, the policy collapses to "keep everything"
because the reward gradient favors it even when the answer doesn't change.

We rewrote the reward to symmetrize the signal, which moved the policy off the
all-keep fixed point in the first 200 episodes.

### 2. Faithfulness mismatch vs. ground truth

If we compare the compressed-prompt answer to ground-truth, short answers with
high surface overlap look faithful even when the reasoning changed. Comparing
to the *uncompressed* model's answer instead fixes this: the reward actually
measures "did compression change the model's behavior?" rather than "did the
model happen to be right?"

<Pullquote>
  The choice of reward function had a larger effect on the
  compression–faithfulness tradeoff than the choice of algorithm.
</Pullquote>

## Results

- **Best config**: 74% compression while preserving 90–95% of task F1 on
  SQuAD 2.0.
- **Algorithm vs. reward**: across the 2×2×2 factorial, reward choice moved
  the Pareto frontier more than algorithm choice did.
- **Sample efficiency**: MCTS with the cross-entropy dense reward at 2,000
  episodes surpassed a 4,000-episode harmonic-reward baseline across every
  metric — roughly 2× sample efficiency from reward design alone.

## What I'd explore next

- A learned faithfulness verifier (instead of reference-answer comparison) as
  the reward.
- Transfer to multi-turn dialogue where surface overlap is a weaker proxy for
  faithfulness.
- Curriculum over compression ratio — train with a moving compression target
  instead of fixing it up front.
